What is an adult family home?

The basics: how adult family homes work in Washington, who they serve and how they differ from assisted living.

Harmony Living House5 min readWashington State

If you're starting to look at care options for a parent or spouse in Washington, you'll quickly hear the term adult family home, often shortened to AFH. Here's what it means and why many families choose one.

A home, licensed for care

An adult family home is a regular residential house that is licensed by the Washington State Department of Social and Health Services (DSHS) to care for a small number of adults. Most homes care for up to six residents, and some are approved for up to eight. Residents receive a room, meals, laundry, supervision and help with personal care.

Who lives in an adult family home?

AFH residents are usually older adults or adults with disabilities who can no longer live safely on their own but don't need a hospital. Common reasons include:

  • Needing help with bathing, dressing, toileting or moving around
  • Trouble managing medications safely
  • Memory loss or dementia that makes living alone risky
  • Recovery after a hospital stay, or a decline in health over time

How it compares with other options

Assisted living facilities are larger buildings with many apartments. Nursing homes provide skilled nursing around the clock. An adult family home sits in between: a small, family-style setting with 24-hour care and much more personal attention.

The biggest difference families notice is the ratio: a few residents, the same familiar caregivers and a real kitchen table.

Every resident has a care plan

Washington requires each AFH to write a Negotiated Care Plan with the resident and their representative. It describes the help the person needs, their preferences and who does what. The plan is completed within 30 days of moving in and updated when needs change.

How homes are overseen

DSHS licenses and inspects adult family homes, and you can look up any home's license and inspection history online. Residents also have access to the free Long-Term Care Ombuds program for advocacy and support.

This guide is general information for families, not legal, medical or financial advice. Rules and programs change, so please confirm details with the agency involved.
